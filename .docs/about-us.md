## Who We Are

**Belayan Sejahtera Lestari (BESTARI)** is an independent social foundation rooted in the communities of the Belayan River region, East Kalimantan, Indonesia.

Established in Muai Village, Kembang Janggut District, BESTARI was created to support people and communities in building stronger livelihoods, expanding access to knowledge, strengthening local leadership, and protecting the natural environment on which their future depends.

We believe sustainable development must begin with people.

It must understand how communities live, work, learn, produce, and make decisions. It must create opportunities without weakening local identity. And it must improve lives today without reducing the possibilities available to future generations.

BESTARI works with communities, partners, institutions, and networks that share this belief.

---

# Our Story

BESTARI grew from years of experience working closely with farmers, workers, women, young people, community groups, and rural institutions in the Belayan River area.

Its roots are closely connected to the journey of **Koperasi Belayan Sejahtera (KBS)**, a community-based cooperative established to strengthen the economic position and welfare of its members.

Through that journey, an important lesson became clear.

Many challenges faced by rural communities extend beyond economic activity alone.

Education, leadership, environmental protection, climate resilience, women's participation, youth development, research, entrepreneurship, and access to wider opportunities require a broader platform for action.

BESTARI was established to provide that space.

While BESTARI and KBS share historical roots and may continue to collaborate, they are independent institutions with their own governance, responsibilities, and institutional direction.

BESTARI carries forward the spirit from which it emerged while building a wider path toward social and environmental progress.

---

# The Meaning Behind Our Name

Our name reflects where we come from and where we want to go.

**Belayan** represents our roots.

The Belayan River is more than a geographical reference. It connects villages, livelihoods, histories, cultures, and generations. It represents the communities from which BESTARI was born.

**Sejahtera** represents the future we seek.

For us, prosperity is not measured only by income. It includes knowledge, dignity, opportunity, security, participation, and the ability of people to shape their own future.

**Lestari** represents our responsibility.

Development must endure. Economic progress must respect ecological limits. Resources must be managed responsibly. And every generation must leave meaningful opportunities for the next.

Together, these ideas define BESTARI:

**rooted in community, committed to prosperity, and guided by sustainability.**

# Our Mission

BESTARI works to:

**Expand access to knowledge and learning**
by supporting education, capacity building, leadership, and lifelong learning within communities.

**Strengthen community livelihoods**
by supporting farmers, women, youth, community enterprises, and local economic initiatives.

**Advance sustainable development**
by encouraging responsible production, environmental stewardship, climate resilience, and sustainable use of natural resources.

**Create opportunities for the next generation**
by developing the capacity, confidence, creativity, and leadership of young people.

**Support women as agents of change**
by expanding their participation, skills, economic opportunities, and leadership within families and communities.

**Develop knowledge from local experience**
through research, documentation, data, innovation, and the preservation of local knowledge and culture.

**Build meaningful partnerships**
between communities, government, civil society, universities, businesses, financial institutions, donors, and development partners.

# Our Strategic Pillars

## 01

## Education & Human Development

Knowledge creates choices.

BESTARI supports learning that is practical, accessible, and relevant to everyday life.

Our work may include community education, financial literacy, leadership development, vocational skills, organizational capacity building, entrepreneurship, and learning opportunities for young people.

We see education not simply as transferring information, but as strengthening people's ability to understand their circumstances, make better decisions, and create new possibilities.

---

## 02

## Community Economy & Sustainable Livelihoods

A strong community needs a strong economic foundation.

BESTARI works with smallholder farmers, women, youth, community groups, cooperatives, and micro and small enterprises to strengthen their ability to generate sustainable livelihoods.

Our work may include business development, financial management, product development, market access, access to finance, institutional strengthening, agricultural improvement, and value-chain development.

Our objective is not to create permanent dependence on assistance.

It is to strengthen the capacity of people and institutions to grow independently.

---

## 03

## Environment, Climate & Sustainable Agriculture

The future of rural communities is inseparable from the future of their environment.

BESTARI supports initiatives that strengthen environmental stewardship, sustainable agriculture, climate resilience, responsible land management, biodiversity protection, waste management, and community awareness of environmental risks.

We believe prosperity and environmental sustainability should not be treated as competing goals.

The challenge is to build systems in which both can advance together.

---

## 04

## Research, Knowledge & Local Culture

Communities are not only recipients of knowledge.

They are producers of knowledge.

Farmers understand their land. Women understand household economies. Communities understand their history, culture, environment, and local institutions.

BESTARI seeks to document and develop this knowledge while connecting it with research, technology, data, and wider networks.

Through research, documentation, discussion, publication, and knowledge exchange, we aim to make local experience visible, useful, and influential beyond the communities where it originated.

---

# Our Priority Communities

BESTARI works with communities while giving particular attention to groups whose potential is often greater than the opportunities available to them.

These include:

**Smallholder farmers**, particularly those navigating changing markets, sustainability standards, environmental pressures, and increasingly complex agricultural systems.

**Women**, whose economic and social contributions are fundamental to rural communities but are not always matched by equal access to opportunities and decision-making.

**Young people**, who need meaningful pathways to learn, create, lead, and build futures within and beyond their communities.

**Micro and small entrepreneurs**, whose businesses can strengthen household incomes and create more resilient local economies.

**Community groups and local institutions**, whose strength often determines whether development initiatives continue after external support ends.

# Where We Work

BESTARI is headquartered in **Muai Village, Kembang Janggut District, Kutai Kartanegara, East Kalimantan**.

Our primary field of work begins in communities along the **Belayan River and surrounding areas**.

This is where we have our deepest relationships, strongest understanding, and most immediate responsibility.

But our ambition is not limited by geography.

We believe lessons developed from local experience can contribute to wider conversations about sustainable agriculture, rural development, community empowerment, climate resilience, and inclusive economic development across Indonesia.

Our long-term vision is national.

Our roots remain in Belayan.

# How We Work

## We Listen Before We Design

Every community is different.

We begin by understanding people, local institutions, existing resources, challenges, and opportunities before designing interventions.

---

## We Work With, Not For

Communities are not passive beneficiaries.

They are partners.

Whenever possible, programs are designed, implemented, reviewed, and improved together with the people they are intended to serve.

---

## We Build Capacity, Not Dependency

A successful program should eventually need less external support, not more.

We therefore prioritize knowledge, leadership, organizational strength, and local ownership.

---

## We Connect Local Strength with Wider Opportunity

Many communities already possess skills, resources, knowledge, and initiative.

What is often missing is access.

BESTARI helps connect local potential with markets, finance, research, technology, policy networks, institutions, and development partners.

---

## We Learn from Evidence

Good intentions are not enough.

We aim to understand what works, what does not, and why.

Programs should be guided by evidence, monitored responsibly, and improved through continuous learning.

# Our Principles

## Independence

## Inclusion

## Respect

## Sustainability

## Collaboration

government institutions,
universities and research institutions,
civil society organizations,
cooperatives and community organizations,
private-sector partners,

# Accountability

BESTARI recognizes that a social institution is built on public trust.

We therefore seek to develop strong systems for governance, financial accountability, program monitoring, partnership management, and responsible decision-making.

As the organization grows, our institutional systems must grow with it.

We are committed to building an organization where resources can be traced, programs can be evaluated, decisions can be explained, and lessons can be openly learned.

Our ambition is not simply to implement more programs.

It is to build an institution worthy of long-term trust.

# Our Ambition

BESTARI begins in Belayan, but the questions we work on are much larger.

How can rural communities create prosperity without losing control over their resources?

How can smallholder farmers participate more fairly in increasingly global supply chains?

How can economic development strengthen rather than weaken the environment?

How can women and young people gain greater influence over the future of their communities?

How can local knowledge contribute to national development?

And how can communities become strong enough that development is something they shape rather than something that simply arrives?

These are the questions that will guide our journey.
